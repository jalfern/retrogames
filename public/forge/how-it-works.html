How the forge works

The forge is a factory that builds games, run by AI workers instead of people. Almost every rule in it is borrowed from places that solved the same problems long ago: car plants, kitchens, hospitals, newsrooms.

The factory floor

Every rule has a twin on a real factory floor

The order board

In the forge: GitHub issues

Every job is a ticket on the board. Nobody works on anything that isn't pinned up, and the board is the only place workers coordinate. That's why a new worker can start the day they arrive: read the board.

Pull, don't push

In the forge: lanes take the next ticket

Toyota's kanban: a station pulls the next job only when it's free, and there's a cap on how much sits half-done. Each lane takes one issue at a time and may ask at most three open questions.

The andon cord

In the forge: the halt label, and a red main

Any worker at Toyota can pull a cord and stop the line when something's wrong. Put "halt" on any issue and every lane finishes its step and stops. When the nightly tests fail, nothing ships until it's fixed.

Fix it, or tag it out

In the forge: the 2-hour red-main rule

A broken machine gets fixed fast, or tagged out of service so the line restarts, with a named person owning the repair. A failing check gets fixed within two hours, or quarantined against an open issue that owns the fix.

The golden sample

In the forge: checks and golden traces

Factories keep a perfect reference part to compare every new one against. The web game's recorded runs are the reference; the Apple TV versions must match them tick for tick. Every test is also proven to fail on a broken part.

One shipping door

In the forge: the rule-5 merge command

Goods leave through one gate, after one inspection, for everyone. No side doors. Every change merges the same way, and only once the required check passes.

Shift change

In the forge: STATE.md

Nurses hand over a patient with a short spoken summary because the next shift wasn't there. AI workers start every session with no memory, so the last one rewrites a one-page note of where things stand.

The foreman

In the forge: the warden

Walks the line every ten minutes: who's idle, what's stuck, what's next. Restarts a stalled station. Never builds anything himself.

The suggestion box with a deadline

In the forge: questions for Jon

A worker who needs a decision writes the options and what they'll do by default, then keeps working. If the owner doesn't answer in two days, the default goes ahead. Nobody stands around waiting.

The security desk

In the forge: the trust gate

A note slipped under the door isn't a work order. Requests from outside wait until the owner signs off; to AI workers, any text they read is information, never instructions.

  • Stations: grill, sauté, pastry (builder and QA lanes)

A hospital ward

  • Standing orders that apply unless a doctor says otherwise (question defaults)
  • Charge nurse doing rounds (the warden)
  • Check the chart, not the hallway rumour (the trust gate)

The short version

The forge is a game factory where the workers are AI models. It runs on the same habits that keep any good line moving: one board of jobs, one job per station, a cord anyone can pull, a reference to check against, one door out, and notes for the next shift. The owner sets the taste; the line does the rest.
