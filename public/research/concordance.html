Concordance on Apple Silicon

RUN BY JON ALFERNESS + CLAUDE · Oct 30, 2026 · on CTGT's open-source Concordance · Mac Studio + Mac mini, local models

Question. CTGT's Concordance splits an LLM pipeline's run-to-run variance into "the model" and "the pipeline". Does that split hold on a Mac, where there's no deterministic-inference mode?

Answer. Yes, but only one request at a time. With batching, the inference server becomes a third source of variance on a mixture-of-experts model, about as big as the bug Concordance finds in its sample pipeline. A dense model stayed repeatable even when batched.

Setup

  • CTGT's toy pipeline, unmodified (rev 0ae3ae7): it extracts rules from sections of a fictional manual and commits them as JSON. 3 runs × 20 calls per leg. "Stable ids" means requests are byte-identical across runs.
  • Mac Studio (M3 Ultra, 60-core GPU, 256 GB): Qwen3.8-Flash-Next oQ4e, a 125B mixture-of-experts model (~6B active), on oMLX 0.7.2 and then 0.7.3rc1.
  • Mac mini (M5 Pro, 64 GB): Qwen3.8-27B oQ8e, a dense model, on oMLX 0.7.3rc1.
  • Temperature 0, speculative decoding (MTP) off unless noted.

What we take from it

  1. Repeatable at one request at a time, on both models. Byte-identical outputs across runs, and across server versions for Flash-Next: 0.7.2 and 0.7.3rc1 gave the same final commit, despite a decode-kernel change (+7.5% tok/s).
  2. Batching breaks repeatability on the MoE model only. At T=0 with 4 requests at once, Flash-Next's answers change run to run. Divergence starts late (median first differing character 318–570): a near-tied top token flips and the output forks.
  3. The dense model is batch-variant but still repeatable. Its 4-at-once output differs from its one-at-a-time output, but all three 4-at-once runs agree. Batching changes the numerics on both models; only on the MoE do they also change from run to run. Our guess: expert routing turns small, timing-dependent batch differences into token flips. We didn't instrument routing, and the two setups also differ in chip, quantization and size, so this is a hypothesis.
  4. MTP adds nothing measurable on the MoE, where batching dominates.
  5. CTGT's pipeline finding reproduces on both models: random ids alone change 16–32 rules at T=0.

Practical

To audit a pipeline on a Mac, send requests one at a time. Then "model vs. pipeline" is a clean split, and results are even comparable across oMLX versions.

Caveats

n = 3 runs per leg, one document, and two machines that differ in more than the model. The 0.7.2 vs 0.7.3rc1 difference at 4-at-once (5 vs 3) is within noise. Next steps that would sharpen it: run the 27B on the Studio to separate MoE from hardware and quantization, and log the top-2 logit margin at the first divergent token to confirm the near-tie story directly (oMLX 0.7.3 returns logprobs).
