WEIGHT SURGERYREAD ▸

Can a local model fix another model by editing its weights directly, with no training? MIT had GPT-6 Astra cut Qwen3.5-0.8B's reasoning loops by changing 0.016% of its weights. We tried to reproduce that on a Mac and to have our own Qwen find an edit.

MIT's edit reproduces on a Mac at about ⅔ strength (37% on MATH vs MIT's 45.8%, same 100 problems), once two hidden bugs were fixed. Qwen's own blind edit doubles the base model (6% → 12%); building an edit from MIT's written recipe does no better than base (5%). What makes MIT's edit work is in the weights it chose, not in the recipe's words.

HAND-WRITTEN WEIGHTSPLAY ▸

A small neural net plays the arcade's real Arkanoid with every weight written by hand: no data, no gradients. Live heatmaps show what each unit reads; a slider rewrites 16 weights mid-game.

The 4,485-weight chaser dropped one ball in 20 games; a hand-written "aim at the bricks" edit lifts the median score 8,324 → 11,326, but the hand-coded autopilot still scores 18,994.

CONCORDANCE ON APPLE SILICONREAD ▸

CTGT's open-source Concordance splits an LLM pipeline's run-to-run variance into "the model" and "the pipeline". Does that hold on a Mac with no deterministic-inference mode?

Yes, one request at a time, even across server versions. With batching, the server becomes a third source of variance on a mixture-of-experts model; a dense model stays repeatable.
