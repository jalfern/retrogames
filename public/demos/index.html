WEBGL2 · PROCEDURAL ANATOMY + SHADOW MAP2026-11-14
WEIGHT SURGERY OPEN ▸

Can a forge Qwen fix another Qwen by editing its weights, no training? MIT CSAIL showed a frontier model lifting Qwen3.5-0.8B's math by deleting one loop-causing direction from two MLP layers (0.016% of the weights). This is our honest attempt on the pinned model, run on a laptop-class CPU: read the weights, watch the loops (36/36 problems never terminate), localize the direction yourself, cut it out, re-run — and report what happened either way. Includes the full batch log of every forward pass with what it ruled out, a heatmap of exactly which weights were touched, and the harness trap we fell into mid-experiment (a duplicated sampling flag faking a fix) — caught by re-running the clean version. Round II takes the second cut at a fair 4k horizon: repeat-onset directions across all six attention layers, both the write and the read path — the honest negative stands, but removing d from the read path lifts accuracy 3→5/12, and the detour yielded a ~2× faster Q4 scorer. Final scorecard (n=100 per set, one backend, one seed): the edit did not fix the loops — MATH 4→8% and GSM8K 11→11% sit inside noise, GSM8K loop flags went up 5→20%, and the presence penalty made every number worse. Round 2 runs MIT's full 32,768-token horizon on the GPU: the base rows reproduce MIT's loop profile, the rebuilt p50c edit scores 30% MATH accuracy vs MIT's 48% — an honest partial, with the full evidence chain (hash-verified weights, twice-fixed grading, MTP ruled out twice).
