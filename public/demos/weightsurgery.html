WEIGHT SURGERY

Qwen3.5-0.8B @ 2fc06364 — an attempt at weight-space programming (#1493): no gradients, practice on GSM8K/MATH train only, ≤50 forward-pass batches, one scored held-out run. By the forge, on a laptop-class CPU (this box's GPU path is dead, #737).

THE BUG

Qwen's own model card (L856) warns Qwen3.5-0.8B falls into thinking loops. Measured on 36 GSM8K/MATH train problems at a 1024-token cap with the card's own sampling: 36/36 hit the cap. Zero terminated. Even "What is 17×23?" rambles past 512 tokens without an answer.

THE FIX ATTEMPT — the Astra recipe, our own localization

MIT CSAIL (Takehi et al., Nov 2026) fixed this by subtracting one direction written by the MLPs of layers 15 & 19 from 64 down_proj columns in each — 0.016% of the weights. We localized our own direction d: the mean residual-stream write of layers 15/19 across 9,220 looping positions (captured by prefill only, train splits), then removed d's component from the top-64 / top-256 / all 3,584 down_proj columns of those two layers (0.017% / 0.070% / 0.98% of weights).

VERDICT — a clean negative, and a harness trap we fell into and caught

The Astra recipe did not reproduce with our budget and our localization. Removing our loop-write direction from layers 15/19 — even from all columns (0.98% of weights) — left 12/12 practice problems non-terminating; the ablated model stayed coherent but rambled longer. Within a 1024-token horizon, 9,220 "looping" positions look like verbose self-questioning, not the tight attractor MIT traced over 32k-token horizons: what we could localize at this scale isn't the thing they cut.

The control row caught us out. An early run looked like the model-card fix (presence penalty 2.0) suddenly terminating 12/12 — too good. It was a harness artifact: the override flag was appended after the standard 1.5, so the command carried --presence-penalty 1.5 --presence-penalty 2.0. With the override applied cleanly (single flag), termination collapsed to 0/12. Held-out scoring, run before we noticed, shows the same: presence 2.0 still hits the cap on 95% of MATH and 100% of GSM8K problems.

Bottom line: on a laptop CPU, at a 1024-token cap, with a whole-sequence mean direction — neither weight surgery nor the model card's own sampling settings break the non-termination. MIT's numbers stand (their logs are public); reproducing them needs their horizon (32k), their batch sizes, and probably their localization method (repeat-onset contrast, not sequence mean). Next: capture at 2–8k horizons from the moment loops start, ablate through o_proj/unembed too, score full MATH-500, then compare against MIT's published edit — in that order.
